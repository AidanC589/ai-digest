General AI Developments

Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Meta, Microsoft, and Mistral all signed the EU Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-Generated Content
All major labs are now committed to watermarking AI-generated content including code and text, even from open-source models; this is now a legal requirement, not a choice, which directly affects AI-generated output you ship.
Stealing Reasoning Traces from Proprietary LLM APIs
Researchers found that encrypted chain-of-thought blocks from GPT and Claude could be replayed into weaker sibling models and jailbroken to expose raw reasoning in plaintext; Claude Haiku 4.5 was the most vulnerable, and the attack also enabled prompt injection via poisoned reasoning traces. Now patched across all providers.

Engineering & AI Workflows

Go is an ideal language for AI-assisted software engineering
Google argues that Go's opinionated simplicity, built-in formatter, and strong consistency make it easier for AI agents to generate correct, reviewable code — and easier for humans to review it. The core insight: language choice now matters more for *reading and verifying* AI output than for writing speed.
When Chain-of-Thought Helps and When It Hurts: An Empirical Investigation of the Serial-Depth Bottleneck in LLM Reasoning
Empirical study across three models finds CoT gives +54–68pp recovery on deep reasoning tasks (math, GSM8K) but is largely redundant for shallow tasks (MMLU, ARC). Practically: don't default to CoT in every prompt — save it for multi-step logic and skip it for factual lookups.
There are no lossless transformations of natural-language text
Sophie Alpert's internal policy on AI writing: every sentence in AI-assisted docs must represent your actual thoughts, because every LLM rewrite loses information the model doesn't have. The "I can't explain it, AI wrote it" failure mode is now a documented engineering anti-pattern worth enforcing in code review and PR doc standards.
Company Offering '100% Human-Written, Never AI' Medical Research Is 100% AI
A medical research services company used entirely AI-generated fake staff, impersonated real researchers without consent, and ran AI customer service denying being AI. Directly relevant as a case study in AI-generated output trust and provenance — the exact failure mode that watermarking mandates (above) are designed to address.
