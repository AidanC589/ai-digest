Chain-of-Models: Cross-Model Auditing for Bias-Robust LLM Judges

Engineering & AI Workflows

Don't credit the LLM
Argues that crediting LLM tools (e.g. "Claude wrote this PR") dilutes your own accountability without adding information, the same way a pilot doesn't announce avionics software over the PA. The sharper point: volunteering that an LLM wrote something often signals the work wasn't reviewed — which is the real problem.
Chain-of-Models: Cross-Model Auditing for Bias-Robust LLM Judges
Practical takeaway for code review workflows: using a cross-family model to audit another model's reasoning catches biases that prompt-based debiasing misses, and GPT-4o and GLM-5 are the strongest auditors for different bias types — worth considering when building multi-model review pipelines.
My personal AI benchmark: "Generate an SVG of a frog with a Habsburg jaw"
A well-designed personal benchmark for comparing models on a creative-spatial task (SVG generation with a specific anatomical feature) is a useful template: pick something verifiable, visually inspectable, and slightly weird enough that models can't pattern-match from training data.
Can LLMs Really Understand Item Difficulty Levels?
GPT-5.4 systematically underestimates difficulty and treats most items as easy; zero-shot GPT-4.1 at temperature 0 still scores below a fine-tuned encoder model (QWK 0.578 vs 0.625). Direct implication: don't rely on LLMs to self-assess or calibrate the difficulty of tasks they generate — human or encoder-based validation is needed.
