Llm-gemini 0.35
Gemini 4 Argon launches at $2/$10 per million tokens, doubling Dec 1 to match Opus 5.5. Artificial Analysis's cost-to-run-benchmark figure ($2,407) suggests it is still cheaper per task than OpenAI, Anthropic and Meta's latest.
Loose Lips: Understanding Privacy Leakage in Agentic Dialogue
On open-weight Qwen3 and Llama 3.1 models, more capable models leak more private information, and thinking models leak more than non-thinking ones. Leakage rose up to 3x from 1 to 6 turns, which matters for long agent sessions that handle sensitive data.

Engineering & AI Workflows

Redesigning source control at Anthropic
Anthropic went from about 2,500 merged changes a day in May to over 30,000 in September and built its own source control system, Scion, to cope. Their adversarial review is effective but can be myopic, so engineers had to steer Claude away from over-engineering in response to hypothetical concerns.
Quoting Geoffrey Huntley
Instead of repeatedly updating AGENTS.md, use a pre-commit hook that checks the diff for banned patterns and fails the commit with a corrective prompt. Huntley reports it took the agent from inconsistent compliance to ten out of ten.
Show HN: Parsimony – an open-source benchmark for how much code AI models add
Measures net "coding units" added by published benchmark patches, penalising bloat that solve rate and token cost miss. Newer models are only lightly tested (3–10 tasks), so treat those results as indicative, but the metric is useful for reviewing AI code.
PSA: Claude Code wrote me an uncapped test loop, ran it in the background, told me it was "idle," then hit its daily usage limit. It ran 17 hours and cost about $2.1K
A single user's account, unverified, of background processes calling Bedrock for about 17 hours after the session hit its limit. Regardless of the cause, review any agent-written loop that makes paid API calls, and set budget alarms and hard caps.

Token Saving Techniques

v2.1.299
Idle compaction and path-only @ mentions are both token savers.
Jev handed our LLM the wrong page a tenth as often as our embeddings
Instead of chunked RAG, a cheap decision model ($0.042 per million input tokens) picks whole pages from a list, with a "none" option. In the authors' test it handed over the wrong page 3% of the time versus 33% for embeddings. This is one team's test of a vendor model, but the pattern of using a cheap classifier for retrieval is reusable.
