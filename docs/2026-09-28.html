Engineering & AI Workflows

Calling the AI bluff: "Do not guess" cut made-up claims from 71% to 20%
A simple instruction addition ("Use null for any field whose value is not on the page. Do not guess.") cut hallucinated extraction fields from 70.7% to 20.2% across 16 models; a cheap secondary model checking each returned value caught most remaining fabrications for under a cent per run — a pattern directly reusable for verifying AI-generated outputs.
Auditing and Repairing LLM-as-Judge Failures in a Production Text-to-SQL Pipeline
A production LLM-judge (gpt-4o-mini) agreed with human graders at only kappa=0.04 on hard cases, mostly due to a "grade-hallucination" failure mode; swapping to a self-hosted Qwen model matched a much pricier model's agreement at 1/300th the cost, and unanimity-routed ensembles of three strong judges hit kappa=0.79 — useful reference if you rely on LLM review gates.
Quoting Muse AI Agent
A real transcript of an autonomous agent's auto-reply falsely confirming availability and causing a bad customer interaction — a concrete reminder that giving agents unsupervised "confirm" actions needs guardrails, not just better prompts.
Bootstrapping Conversational Recommendation Agents At Spotify
Spotify's self-improvement loop (variance-based contrastive optimization + a coding agent that auto-fixes planning/tool-use errors) beat a heavily hand-tuned prompt by +8% quality and drove +14% listening and +5% WAU in A/B tests — a good template for iterating agent prompts at scale.
Adding logit penalty for "wait", "maybe" and "perhaps" to Qwen models improves their accuracy
Penalizing "overthinking" hedge tokens during reasoning improved MATH-500 accuracy across quantization levels (e.g., BF16 74%→84%) while cutting reasoning-token length ~11–19%, suggesting hedge-word suppression as a cheap accuracy/efficiency lever for reasoning models.
