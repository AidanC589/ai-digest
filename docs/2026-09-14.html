General AI Developments

AI recursive self-improvement might not come so quickly after all
Princeton-led researchers tested Claude Opus 4.8 agents against unpublished NeurIPS papers; agents could run hundreds of experiments and write up results, but produced work both original authors rejected — they lacked the judgment to pick good hypotheses or know when to abandon a bad direction.
Garry Tan wants US open-weight AI labs to 'distill' frontier models, too
Amid Anthropic's allegations of "illicit distillation attacks" by Chinese labs, YC's CEO argues US open-weight labs should be free to distill frontier models too, framing model concentration in one closed provider as the bigger long-term risk.

Engineering & AI Workflows

GAUGE: When Not to Trust LLM-as-a-Judge in User-Simulated Evaluation of Task-Oriented Agents
Testing 25 agents across six providers, researchers found LLM-judge satisfaction scores are essentially uncorrelated with actual task success (57.5% of "satisfied" conversations had failed tasks), and judge rankings become unreliable — a 31% disagreement rate — exactly when comparing near-equal agents. Directly relevant if you're using LLM judges to gate agent/PR quality.
commit-rewriter 0.1
Simon Willison's new tool rewrites commit messages to strip out "coding agent cruft" and internal issue references before pushing to public repos, with automatic backup branching for safe reverts — a practical fix for a common AI-assisted-dev cleanup problem.
Local Edits, Global Ripples: Replay-Informed Policy Adaptation for Workflow Synthesis
A new method (RIPPLE) for editing agent prompts/policies shows that seemingly isolated prompt edits can ripple into unrelated downstream behavior, and edits that help in isolation can become harmful once composed with prior fixes — a caution for anyone iteratively patching system prompts or agent instructions.
The Cost of Compression: A Rate-Distortion Limit on Factual Hallucination
A theoretical model shows hallucination isn't just a coverage gap (missing facts) but also a compression problem: even observed facts get stored lossily under finite model capacity, giving a mathematical account of why models confidently misstate things they technically "know."
