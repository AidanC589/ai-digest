General AI Developments

US tech stocks see sharp falls over AI cost fears
Intel fell 28% and Alphabet lost over $500bn in value after saying AI summaries are hurting search ad revenue, while Amazon, Meta, Oracle, Microsoft and Alphabet plan roughly $800bn of 2026 AI spending. The Nasdaq had its worst month since March 2025, and sustained pressure could eventually affect model pricing and subsidies.
PostgreSQL 19 Running Behind Schedule Due to AI/LLM Burden
Maintainers say AI-found bugs and LLM-drafted patches that "aren't always correct or compliant" with project standards still need human review, causing late reverts and a delayed release. This is a concrete example of AI output shifting the burden onto reviewers.

Engineering & AI Workflows

Building an AI-native engineering org
FurtherAI reports about 5x more merged changes per engineer per day since January with a revert rate under 0.5%. The approach: agents write the tests (91% of changes now include them, up from 15%), AI reviews 99% of changes before humans do, and recurring review findings become 21 automated rule checks that agents hit and self-correct against. Up to a third of changes now merge without a person.
Proton's CEO Cancels AI 'Re-Interviews'
Proton's CEO pushed AI-written code live over a security employee's request for a few days of pentesting, telling staff to "stop lovingly handcrafting all our code". It's a cautionary tale about skipping security review on AI-generated code, and it triggered employee and user backlash.
AI isn't replacing SREs, it's deskilling them
Applies Bainbridge's "Ironies of Automation" to argue that automating routine work erodes the skills needed for the rare, hard failures humans still own. The same logic applies to engineers who only review AI-written code.
Ask HN: Are you tired too in this AI era?
A highly discussed thread (139 comments) on the mental load of parallel agents, reading large volumes of generated output, and constant context switching. Useful for spotting review-fatigue risks in your own workflow.
