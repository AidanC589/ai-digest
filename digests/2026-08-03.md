## Engineering & AI Workflows
- [Don't credit the LLM](https://isaacsu.com/2026/08/dont-credit-the-llm/) — Argues that crediting LLM tools (e.g. "Claude wrote this PR") dilutes your own accountability without adding information, the same way a pilot doesn't announce avionics software over the PA. The sharper point: volunteering that an LLM wrote something often signals the work wasn't reviewed — which is the real problem.
- [Chain-of-Models: Cross-Model Auditing for Bias-Robust LLM Judges](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28636) — Practical takeaway for code review workflows: using a cross-family model to audit another model's reasoning catches biases that prompt-based debiasing misses, and GPT-4o and GLM-5 are the strongest auditors for different bias types — worth considering when building multi-model review pipelines.
- [My personal AI benchmark: "Generate an SVG of a frog with a Habsburg jaw"](https://frogs.vaguespac.es/) — A well-designed personal benchmark for comparing models on a creative-spatial task (SVG generation with a specific anatomical feature) is a useful template: pick something verifiable, visually inspectable, and slightly weird enough that models can't pattern-match from training data.
- [Can LLMs Really Understand Item Difficulty Levels?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28634) — GPT-5.4 systematically underestimates difficulty and treats most items as easy; zero-shot GPT-4.1 at temperature 0 still scores below a fine-tuned encoder model (QWK 0.578 vs 0.625). Direct implication: don't rely on LLMs to self-assess or calibrate the difficulty of tasks they generate — human or encoder-based validation is needed.

## Try This
Use a cross-family model as an explicit auditor in your AI code review loop. After Claude Code generates a PR or a complex function, pipe the reasoning/explanation it produces into a different-family model (e.g. GPT-4o or Gemini) with the prompt: "Here is an AI model's explanation of the code changes it made. Identify any reasoning gaps, unstated assumptions, or logic that doesn't match the actual diff." The Chain-of-Models research shows that auditor diversity — not auditor quality in isolation — is what catches biases and errors that a single-model review misses.
