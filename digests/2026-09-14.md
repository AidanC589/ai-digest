# AI Digest — 2026-09-14

## TL;DR
A new multi-institution study found AI agents can execute the mechanics of research but still lack the judgment to produce genuinely novel, publication-worthy work — a useful reality check if you're relying on agents for anything beyond well-specified engineering tasks. Separately, a paper on LLM-as-judge evaluation shows it gets unreliable exactly when you need it most: comparing two closely-matched agents. On the tooling side, Simon Willison shipped a small utility for cleaning up AI-generated commit message cruft before publishing, and a new desktop IDE (AgentsDock) lets you run Claude Code, Codex, and Cursor side-by-side from your phone.

## General AI Developments
- [AI recursive self-improvement might not come so quickly after all](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/08/18/1142188/ai-recursive-self-improvement/) — Princeton-led researchers tested Claude Opus 4.8 agents against unpublished NeurIPS papers; agents could run hundreds of experiments and write up results, but produced work both original authors rejected — they lacked the judgment to pick good hypotheses or know when to abandon a bad direction.
- [Garry Tan wants US open-weight AI labs to 'distill' frontier models, too](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/11/y-combinators-garry-tan-wants-u-s-open-weight-ai-labs-to-distill-frontier-models-too/) — Amid Anthropic's allegations of "illicit distillation attacks" by Chinese labs, YC's CEO argues US open-weight labs should be free to distill frontier models too, framing model concentration in one closed provider as the bigger long-term risk.

## Engineering & AI Workflows
- [GAUGE: When Not to Trust LLM-as-a-Judge in User-Simulated Evaluation of Task-Oriented Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12191) — Testing 25 agents across six providers, researchers found LLM-judge satisfaction scores are essentially uncorrelated with actual task success (57.5% of "satisfied" conversations had failed tasks), and judge rankings become unreliable — a 31% disagreement rate — exactly when comparing near-equal agents. Directly relevant if you're using LLM judges to gate agent/PR quality.
- [commit-rewriter 0.1](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/14/commit-rewriter/) — Simon Willison's new tool rewrites commit messages to strip out "coding agent cruft" and internal issue references before pushing to public repos, with automatic backup branching for safe reverts — a practical fix for a common AI-assisted-dev cleanup problem.
- [Local Edits, Global Ripples: Replay-Informed Policy Adaptation for Workflow Synthesis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12127) — A new method (RIPPLE) for editing agent prompts/policies shows that seemingly isolated prompt edits can ripple into unrelated downstream behavior, and edits that help in isolation can become harmful once composed with prior fixes — a caution for anyone iteratively patching system prompts or agent instructions.
- [The Cost of Compression: A Rate-Distortion Limit on Factual Hallucination](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12111) — A theoretical model shows hallucination isn't just a coverage gap (missing facts) but also a compression problem: even observed facts get stored lossily under finite model capacity, giving a mathematical account of why models confidently misstate things they technically "know."
