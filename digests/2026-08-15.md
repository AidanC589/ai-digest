## Engineering & AI Workflows
- [Don't classify. Hallucinate!](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/14/dont-classify-hallucinate/) — Instead of feeding an LLM a massive list of existing tags/labels and asking it to pick, prompt it to *invent* plausible tags freely, then use vector embeddings to map those hallucinated tags to your real taxonomy. Cleaner prompts, better results at scale — directly applicable to any AI classification or categorisation task in a codebase.
- [A Contract-Grade Verifier for LLM-Generated GPU Kernels](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12700) — Research on formal verification specifically targeting LLM-generated GPU code, addressing a high-risk area where AI-generated output is hard to review manually and bugs are silent. Flag for anyone using AI to generate CUDA or similar low-level compute code.

## Try This
Use the "hallucinate then match" pattern from Doug Turnbull's post for any AI task involving large vocabularies. Instead of pasting your full list of API endpoints, error codes, component names, or module tags into a prompt, ask Claude to freely invent what the answer *should* look like, then programmatically match those outputs against your real list using embeddings or fuzzy search. This keeps your prompts short, avoids overwhelming the context window, and often produces better results because the model isn't anchored to your existing vocabulary. Try it this week on any classification or routing task where you'd normally dump a long enum into the prompt.
